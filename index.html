What I do /

I build models that predict how radio signals travel. Physics sets the structure, deep learning fills in the detail, and the result runs fast enough to use.

My research covers terrain, indoor and tunnel propagation, with three first or co-first author papers. Before that I shipped computer vision on edge hardware at builderX Robotics. The long-term aim is aerospace and space communications.

01 Research, first author

Physics-guided deep learning for terrain RF propagation

A neural surrogate that predicts path loss over terrain profiles, guided by propagation physics. It outperforms a 27M-parameter U-Net, runs more than 2000 times faster than split-step parabolic equation solvers, and generalises zero-shot to frequencies it never saw in training.

  • Under review, IEEE Transactions on Antennas and Propagation
  • Over 2000x faster than SSPE
  • Beats a 27M-parameter U-Net
  • Zero-shot to unseen frequencies
  • Advised by Prof. Hao Qin
Read on arXiv (2611.32698)

03 Research, first author

Indoor wall geometry from sparse 5G signals

A two-stage training method that infers where walls are inside a building using only sparse 5G RSSI measurements, raising Wall F1 from 0.166 to 0.792.

  • Under review, IEEE Wireless Communications Letters
  • Wall F1 0.166 to 0.792
04 Research, co-first author

Coarse-to-fine reconstruction for tunnel propagation

A model that first captures the overall shape of a signal field inside a tunnel, then refines the fine detail, for accurate propagation prediction in confined spaces.

  • Accepted, ICCEM 2026
05 Industry, builderX Robotics

Computer vision on edge hardware

As a computer vision algorithm engineer intern, I deployed SPNet point-cloud reconstruction at under 80 ms per frame on NVIDIA Jetson, and built a YOLOv5 to TensorRT detection pipeline with precision and recall above 0.98.

  • Under 80 ms per frame
  • Precision and recall above 0.98
  • NVIDIA Jetson, TensorRT
